A slower sales period is a useful prompt to review inventory, but it is not evidence that every store or category is experiencing the same trend. Start with your own product-level sales history, margin, stock age, storage constraints, and upcoming demand rather than a broad market benchmark.

Dead stock ties up cash and operating attention. The right response depends on the product, remaining margin, brand position, seasonality, and whether another sales channel can reach a better-fit customer. The five approaches below are decision options to test, not guaranteed outcomes.

## 1. Run a Time-Boxed, Product-Specific Sale

Choose products using store evidence such as days since last sale, sell-through, weeks of supply, and remaining margin. Avoid discounting the whole catalog just because a subset is slow.

Set a clear start and end, explain the offer accurately, and choose a markdown that remains acceptable for the business. There is no universal duration or discount percentage that works across products. Review the result against a written baseline, including units sold, contribution margin, and whether full-price products were displaced.

Email and on-site promotion can both be useful when the audience and message fit. Segment only with data your store is permitted to use, and avoid false scarcity or unsupported former-price comparisons.

## 2. Test a Complementary Bundle

A slow-moving product may work better when paired with a product customers already understand. The bundle should solve a coherent customer problem rather than hide an unwanted item.

Start with a small, controlled test. Compare bundle margin, conversion, return rate, and whether the bundle merely shifts demand away from the standalone winner. Keep, revise, or stop the bundle based on that evidence.

## 3. Compare Alternative Channels

Marketplace, wholesale, liquidation, and donation paths have different fees, operational requirements, brand effects, and accounting consequences. Compare expected net recovery and workload before acting.

- A marketplace can reach a different buyer but may add fees and fulfillment work.
- A wholesale buyer may prefer a coherent lot rather than individual products.
- A liquidation buyer can reduce handling time, usually in exchange for lower recovery.
- Donation may be appropriate for some goods, but tax treatment is fact-specific and should be checked with a qualified adviser.

## 4. Use Inventory as a Deliberate Promotion Cost

A free gift, sampling program, or giveaway can be reasonable when it reaches the intended customer and the economics are measured. Set eligibility, disclosure, and success criteria before launch. Do not assume that creator reach, engagement, or follower counts translate into sales.

Treat the product cost, fulfillment cost, and staff time as campaign costs. Compare the result with another promotion you could have run using the same resources.

## 5. Create a Repeatable Slow-Inventory Review

The most durable fix is a recurring review with thresholds chosen for the store and category. Useful signals can include:

- days since last sale;
- sell-through and sales-velocity direction;
- weeks of supply;
- remaining seasonal selling window;
- landed cost, storage cost, and margin available for action; and
- supplier, return, donation, or disposal constraints.

No single age threshold defines dead stock for every merchant. Document why an item is flagged, who owns the next decision, and when it will be reviewed again.

StockClearance can help identify slow or dead inventory, show capital at risk, and organize products for review. Merchants still decide and execute any pricing, discount, bundle, collection, or disposal action.

## The Bottom Line

Use store-specific evidence, choose one bounded action, and measure the result. An early, reviewed decision preserves more options than waiting for inventory to become obviously unsellable, but the correct path remains product- and business-specific.
